# Analyzer experiment on cc-news

Run 2026-10-08, re-verified 2026-10-17. Plan: `.claude/plans/analyzer-experiment.md`. Every
number below comes from `out/tables.md` (generated by `scripts/tables.py`; table ids T1 to T11
point there) or from `out/verification.md` (ids such as F1.6). Versions and image digests:
`out/versions.json`.

The re-verification re-ran every stemming and lemmatization claim on fresh containers with the
same images, recomputed the tables from the raw outputs with new code, re-executed the retrieval
runs (300/300 identical rankings for the four chains re-run), and checked each engine claim
against source code at the release tag. It corrected five statements in this report, each
marked *Corrected* below, and one ambiguous sentence in `tables.md` (T10); all are listed in
`out/verification.md`.

## Setup

- Source: `datasets/cc-news/dataset-canonical.jsonl`, sha256 `4b61eb95...b3f76` (matches the
  preprocess report), 707,910 articles.
- Sample A: 10,000 articles, sample B: 50,000 (A is a subset of B), uniform, seed 20261008.
  Fields title, description and content are kept separate throughout.
- Engines, each in a throwaway container: PostgreSQL 18.1 (pg-native), PostgreSQL 18.4 with
  pg_textsearch 1.1.0 (tiger), ParadeDB pg_search 0.21.5 on PostgreSQL 18.1, Elasticsearch
  8.12.0 (Lucene 9.9.1) with analysis-icu.
- References: PyStemmer 2.2.0.3 and 3.0.0, snowballstemmer 2.2.0 and 3.0.1 (pure Python,
  isolated environments), NLTK 3.9.1 Porter in its three modes, spaCy 3.8.7 with
  en_core_web_sm 3.8.0, WordNet 3.0 `morphy`, hunspell en_US from LibreOffice dictionaries
  commit `32b006a2`.
- Each engine's tokens come from that engine: PG `to_tsvector`, ES term vectors read back
  from a real index, tiger's index statistics and match counts, and ParadeDB's `pdb.*` cast.
  The cast output was checked against a bm25 index built with one alias per chain: 10,500
  sampled terms, one mismatch (the emoji `👏🏻` with a skin-tone modifier, present in the cast
  output and not matched in the index; cause not investigated) (T11).
- Determinism: a full second collection produced byte-identical dumps for all 27 chains.

## Findings

### 1. "Porter2" is not one function across the engines (T1)

Fed the same 87,372 words one at a time:

- ES (`snowball` English and `stemmer: porter2`), ParadeDB (`stemmer=english`) and both
  Snowball 2.2.0 references give identical stems on every word.
- PG 18.1 differs from them on 10 words: `added`, `adding`, `anning`, `ebbed`, `ebbing`,
  `egged`, `epping`, `erred`, `erring`, `offing`. PG gives `add`, `ebb`, `err`, `off`; the
  others give `ad`, `eb`, `er`, `of`. Weighted by document frequency over (article,
  field) units, they carry 0.077% of the vocabulary's weight.
- Cause, verified in source: each engine compiles a different snapshot of Snowball's English
  algorithm (F1.3, F1.6).

  | engine | Snowball English snapshot | has the 2023 undoubling change |
  |---|---|---|
  | PG 18.1 and 18.4, so also tiger | commit `d19326ac`, 2025-02-19 (`src/backend/snowball/README`) | yes |
  | ES `snowball` and `stemmer: porter2` (Lucene 9.9.1) | commit `d8cf01dd`, 2020-11-11 (`gradle/generation/snowball.gradle`) | no |
  | ParadeDB 0.21.5 | rust-stemmers 1.2.0, English source imported 2017-02, no release named | no |
  | reference: Snowball 2.2.0 release | 2021-11-10 | no |

  The undoubling change ("Add extra condition to undoubling", 2023-10-27) stops conflating
  add/ad, egg/eg and off/of. `d19326ac` includes it and none of the English changes from
  2025-03-17 on. Snowball's `english.sbl` did not change in content between 2017 and that
  commit, which is why ES, ParadeDB and Snowball 2.2.0 agree on every word although they come
  from three different snapshots. The undoubling change first reached a release in Snowball
  3.0.0 (2025-05-08). *Corrected (F1.6): the first version said the Lucene and ParadeDB
  snapshots were not identified.*
- PG 18.1 and 18.4 stem all 87,372 words identically, so tiger and pg-native share one stemmer
  (F1.4).
- Snowball 3.0 (PyStemmer 3.0.0, snowballstemmer 3.0.1) differs from PG on 84 words and from ES
  on 94, the 84 plus the 10 undoubling words (F1.5): `university` -> `universiti`, `evening`
  kept, `organization` -> `organiz`, `emergency` -> `emergenc`.

So an ES or ParadeDB index and a PG index cannot be stem-identical. A query for `added` matches
documents containing `ad` and `ads` on ES and ParadeDB (all three stem to `ad`), and does
not on PG, where `added` stems to `add` and `ads` to `ad`. This was confirmed with real match
queries on both engines (F1.7).

### 2. tiger analyzes exactly as PG's `english` configuration (T2)

On all three fields: document count, total token count and distinct terms are identical
(content: 10,000 documents, 2,456,678 tokens, 94,943 terms), and all 1,500 sampled words match
the same number of rows. pg_textsearch exposes no per-document tokens, so this is the strongest
check available. It does not cover every word. The source agrees: pg_textsearch 1.1.0 calls
`to_tsvector` with the configured text search configuration.

tiger is not limited to `english`: it accepts any PostgreSQL text search configuration,
including a custom hunspell one, but a custom configuration must be schema-qualified
(`text_config='public.my_config'`); the bare name fails with "does not exist" (F2.2).

### 3. Other identities and non-identities (T1)

- ES `stemmer: english` gives the same output as `porter_stem` on every vocabulary word; both
  are Lucene's `PorterStemFilter`. That output equals NLTK's Porter with Martin Porter's three
  departures from the 1980 paper (0 words differ), not the 1980 algorithm (133 words differ from
  NLTK's original mode, 136 from libstemmer's `porter`, 1.488% of the weight). *Corrected
  (F3.3): the first run reported 132 and 135 because it read the empty stem the 1980 algorithm
  gives `s` as "unchanged".*
- ES `light_english` gives the same output as `kstem` on every vocabulary word.
- hunspell with the same dictionary file gives different lemma sets on PG and ES for 1,818
  words. Both often return several lemmas for one word (PG 4,147 words, ES 2,921), for example
  `dying` -> `dye`, `dying` and `news` -> `new`, `news`.
- hunspell is not a lemmatizer in the linguistic sense. It strips affixes listed for a
  dictionary entry and has no context, so irregular forms stay as they are: `better`, `was`,
  `went`, `mice` and `children` are unchanged on both engines. spaCy, which uses the
  part-of-speech tag, maps `went` -> `go`, `mice` -> `mouse`, `was` -> `be` and `children` ->
  `child` in most occurrences (F3.6).
- hunspell en_US does not know 43,618 of the 87,372 vocabulary words (names, numbers spelled
  out, rare and new words). In PG's hunspell configuration they fall through to `simple` and
  stay unstemmed (F3.7), which is part of why hunspell misses many merges in finding 6.

### 4. Tokenization is the main residual between the engines (T3, T5)

With stemming and stopwords removed from both sides, against PG's parser (`simple` config):

- ES `standard`, ES `icu_tokenizer` and ParadeDB `unicode_words` all implement UAX#29 word
  boundaries. Compared directly with each other (T10), ES `standard` against ParadeDB
  `unicode_words` gives Jaccard 0.99991 (title), 0.999996 (description) and 0.99997 (content).
  The few differences are emoji and symbols (`⚽️`, `♡` kept by ES only), vulgar fractions
  (`½`, `³` kept by ParadeDB only), Thai word segmentation, and the lowercase form of the
  Turkish `İ`. Against PG each scores 0.939 (title), 0.943 (description), 0.935 (content).
- ES `standard` with an apostrophe-to-space char filter is the closest tokenizer to PG:
  0.978, 0.980, 0.970.
- What remains for that chain on content, as a share of the PG-only term units:
  - 57.8% hyphenated compounds: PG indexes `year-old` as well as `year` and `old`.
  - 26.2% numbers: PG splits `4,003` at the comma and keeps signs (`-19` from `COVID-19`,
    `+8`).
  - 12.0% slash compounds and URLs: PG keeps `and/or`, `24/7`, `10/21` whole and emits URL
    host and path tokens.
  - 1.1% emails: PG keeps an address as one token.

  On the ES side the leftovers are numbers ES keeps whole (`1,000`), parts of slash
  compounds, CJK text split one ideograph per token where PG keeps the run, and symbols and
  emoji (`©`, `™`) that PG drops.
- ParadeDB `simple` splits on every non-alphanumeric, so `u.s` becomes `u`, `s` and `3.5`
  becomes `3`, `5`. It matches PG on contractions and loses on dotted and decimal tokens.

None of the tokenizers tested (ES `standard`, `classic`, `letter`, `whitespace`, `icu_tokenizer`,
with and without the apostrophe filter; ParadeDB `unicode_words`, `simple`, `whitespace`, `icu`)
reproduces PG's hyphen, number, slash and email rules. This residual stays in the cross-engine
comparison.

### 5. Full chains against PG's `english` (T4)

| chain | title | description | content |
|---|---|---|---|
| ES `standard` + apostrophe filter + PG's 127 stopwords + snowball | 0.972 | 0.970 | 0.956 |
| ES `standard` + PG's 127 stopwords + snowball | 0.956 | 0.952 | 0.930 |
| ParadeDB `unicode_words` + `stemmer=english` + `stopwords_language=english` | 0.902 | 0.856 | 0.826 |
| ParadeDB `simple` + stemmer + stopwords | 0.891 | 0.847 | 0.827 |
| ES built-in `english` analyzer | 0.839 | 0.775 | 0.756 |

- ParadeDB is held back by stopwords: 78.8% of the terms only ParadeDB indexes are words on
  PG's list that ParadeDB's 33-word English list keeps (`from`, `have`, `has`, `more`, `who`).
- ParadeDB's English stopword list is exactly Lucene's `_english_` (the same 33 words, T10, and
  the tantivy source says it copied Lucene's list). All 33 are on PG's list. ParadeDB applies
  it after stemming (`tokenizers/src/manager.rs`: stemmer, then stopwords), ES and PG before
  (PG's snowball dictionary checks the stop list on the unstemmed word): on ParadeDB `beings` (stem `be`),
  `it's` (stem `it`) and `that's` (stem `that`) are dropped, on ES they are indexed as `be`,
  `it` and `that`. That order is nearly all of the remaining difference between ES `standard` +
  `_english_` + Porter2 and ParadeDB's chain: Jaccard 0.998 (title), 0.995 (description),
  0.995 (content).
  Its `stopwords=` option on the `pdb.*` cast is accepted and ignored: four syntaxes tried,
  and an index built with it still matches `from`. The legacy `paradedb.tokenizer(...,
  stopwords => ...)` function does honour a custom list in `paradedb.tokenize`. Whether 0.21.5
  can build a bm25 index from that legacy configuration was not tested.
- ParadeDB also keeps curly-apostrophe contractions whole and Snowball then strips only the `s`
  (`we’re` -> `we’r`, `batsh’s` -> `batsh’`), 12.4% of its extra terms.
- ES's built-in `english` analyzer is Lucene's chain: standard tokenizer, English possessive
  filter, lowercase, the 33 `_english_` stopwords, Porter. It differs from PG through stopwords,
  the Porter stemmer and the possessive filter (`it's` -> `it`, then dropped as a stopword).
  *Corrected (F5.4): the first version left out the possessive filter.* The `keyword_marker`
  step in ES's documented rebuild is only active when stem exclusions are set. Its documents
  are about 16% longer than PG's (content: 285.4 against 245.7 tokens per article, T9).

### 6. How the stemmers behave on cc-news vocabulary (T6)

35,870 words with document frequency of at least 3. Paice's indices, lower is better: UI counts
missed merges, OI counts wrong merges.

- The three Porter2 versions are indistinguishable at this level (UI 0.079 to 0.080 against
  spaCy, OI 1.92e-5 to 1.93e-5). Their real differences are the 10 to 94 words of finding 1.
- Porter (1980 plus extensions) is close to Porter2: UI 0.082, OI 1.86e-5.
- Krovetz merges far less: UI 0.189 and OI 7.28e-6. It misses more variants and wrongly merges
  about 38% as often as Porter2.
- The S-stemmer handles plurals only: vocabulary reduction 13.9%, UI 0.672.
- Lovins is the most aggressive and the least consistent. It has the highest reduction
  (42.4%), the highest OI (4.21e-5, more than twice Porter's), and still a higher UI than
  Porter (0.145).
- hunspell misses more merges than Krovetz (UI 0.288, between Krovetz and the S-stemmer) and
  wrongly merges about 24% more often than Krovetz (OI 9.03e-6 against 7.28e-6). *Corrected
  (F6.3): the first version said "slightly more often".*
- The two lemma references disagree with each other. Measured against spaCy, WordNet has UI
  0.184; measured against WordNet, spaCy has UI 0.113. spaCy gives 5,609 of the 35,870 words more than one
  lemma in context. There is no single "correct" conflation to measure against.

### 7. Changing the chain changes top-10 results (T7, proxy queries)

300 proxy queries on sample B, all terms required. Mean top-10 Jaccard between two chains on
the same engine. "Identical top 10" means the same ten documents in any order; the share with
the same order as well is lower (for example 37.7% instead of 44.5% for Porter2 against the
S-stemmer). *Corrected (F7.4): the first version did not say order was ignored.*

| engine | change | top-10 Jaccard | identical top 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ES | apostrophe filter only (PG stopwords, snowball on both sides) | 0.954 | 79.0% |
| ES | stopword list only (PG 127 against Lucene 33, Porter2 on both sides) | 0.939 | 73.0% |
| ES | Porter against Porter2 | 0.931 | 76.2% |
| ES | built-in `english` against ES `standard` + PG stopwords + snowball | 0.884 | 57.1% |
| ES | Porter2 against hunspell | 0.823 | 55.5% |
| ES | Porter2 against Krovetz | 0.790 | 50.4% |
| ES | Porter2 against S-stemmer | 0.745 | 44.5% |
| PG | `english` against no stopwords | 0.990 | 98.3% |
| PG | `english` against hunspell | 0.848 | 66.4% |
| PG | `english` against no stemming (`simple`) | 0.684 | 43.6% |

- Stemming families move the results much more than stopword lists or Porter versions.
- The PG stopword row is near 1 for two reasons. The queries contain no stopwords by
  construction, and `ts_rank` with its default normalization ignores document length.
- Without stemming, more queries find nothing: ES 91 of 300 against 67 with Porter2, PG 89
  against 67.

Measured against the rule in `cc-news-parity-spec.md`, which compares the candidates with each
engine's documented setup:

- ES, built-in `english` against the candidates: 0.941 (Porter, Lucene stopwords), 0.930
  (Porter2, Lucene stopwords), 0.884 (Porter2, PG stopwords), 0.832 (hunspell), 0.760
  (Krovetz), 0.741 (S-stemmer).
- PG, `english` against the candidates: 0.990 (no stopwords), 0.848 (hunspell), 0.684 (no
  stemming).
- ParadeDB: not measured (see "Not tested").

The rule never fixed a numeric threshold for "barely move". Calling these divergences large
enough to trigger the judged dev-set step is a judgment made after seeing the data, and the
thesis should say so. Fixing a threshold now, before any dev-set run, would make the next step
rule-based. These numbers say how much results change, not which chain is better.

## What this means for the configuration

**Decided (2026-10-19):** each engine keeps its own tokenizer and stopword list, and only the
stemmer is held constant: Snowball English (Porter2) everywhere. Every engine documents Snowball
as a built-in option, so nothing is borrowed from another engine, and the stemmer, which moves
top-10 results the most (finding 7), stops being a difference between them.

- **ES:** the built-in `english` chain with `snowball` in place of Porter: `standard`
  tokenizer, `possessive_english`, `lowercase`, `_english_` stopwords (33), `snowball` English.
  The nearest measured chain, `es.standard+lucenestop+porter2`, scores 0.900, 0.852 and 0.822
  against PG (T4); it lacks only the possessive filter, which changes possessive forms of
  stopwords such as `it's` (F5.4). The gap to PG is mostly the stopword list (PG 127, Lucene
  33) and is stated as a property of the engine, not corrected.
- **Rejected:** copying PG's 127 stopwords and an apostrophe char filter into ES. It is the
  closest chain measured (0.972, 0.970, 0.956), but no real deployment configures ES that way,
  so the bench would compare a setup nobody runs.
- **ParadeDB** (`stemmer=english`, `stopwords_language=english`) lands where ES does, since
  both use Lucene's 33 stopwords, and could not get closer than about 0.90 anyway until its
  index can take a custom stopword list. `unicode_words` and `simple` are equivalent on content (0.826 against 0.827);
  `unicode_words` is better on titles (0.902 against 0.891) and is output-equivalent to ES
  `standard` up to the edge cases in finding 4.
- **The best arm** needs judgments. In finding 7, changing the stemmer family gives a top-10
  Jaccard of 0.745 to 0.823. Measured directly, 1.07 (hunspell), 1.25 (Krovetz) and 1.48
  (S-stemmer) documents of the top 10 are replaced per query on average (half the symmetric
  difference, F7.5), and only 44.5% to 55.5% of queries keep the same ten documents. Picking
  the stemmer by convention would be a choice that moves the results.

## Not tested, or not certain

- ParadeDB was not part of the retrieval divergence (finding 7), so the first step of the best
  rule is not done for ParadeDB, and the dev-set conclusion holds for ES and PG only. I was not
  confident of an exactly equivalent "every term in any of three fields" query in its API.
- The queries are a proxy: noun chunks from titles outside sample B, no stopwords, AND
  semantics only, on 50,000 articles. Query-side results must be rerun on the real cc-news
  suites.
- ParadeDB's rust-stemmers names no Snowball release; its English source is bounded by its
  2017 import date and the absence of the 2023 change (F1.6). Lucene's snapshot is identified.
- The stem comparison (finding 1) covers lowercase ASCII-alphabetic words only. Words with
  digits or non-ASCII letters were not compared one at a time.
- Paice's indices are computed over word types, unweighted. For hunspell, a word with several
  lemmas keeps itself if it is among them, otherwise the first one. Another rule would change
  hunspell's row in T6.
- spaCy and WordNet are tools with their own errors. spaCy tokenizes differently from PG's
  parser; words are matched by their lowercase surface form.
- Disagreement classes come from a regex on the term. In the tokenizer-only rows the
  `stopword` class means contraction fragments such as `s`, `t` and `don`, not stopword
  filtering. I reviewed 50 random disagreements by hand (`out/review_notes.md`): the class
  matched the term's shape in 50 of 50. The causes for 4 of them are inferred, not seen in the
  text window.
- Paice's indices follow the definitions on Paice's own site and in NLTK's implementation; the
  1994 and 1996 papers themselves were not accessible.
- Analysis throughput was not measured. `_analyze` and term-vector calls time HTTP, not the
  analyzer.
- The ES ICU chain uses `lowercase`, not `icu_normalizer` or `icu_folding`, to stay
  comparable with the other chains.
